Buffalo Chicken (crockpot) CUT ~755 cal / ~114g P whole batch  ·  ~190 cal / ~28g P per serving (4)

Meal prep, made 9/27. ~6.6 cal per gram of protein — denser than the shake. Shreds, reheats, keeps ~4 days.

  • 1 lb chicken breast (545 cal / 102g)
  • ½ cup buffalo sauce (~120 cal)
  • ½ cup cottage cheese, low fat (90 cal / 12g)
  • 1 tbsp dried parsley
  • Method: everything in the crockpot together, ~3 hours on low. Shred and stir. The cottage cheese slow-cooks fine — it melts into the sauce.
  • Serve as lettuce wraps — butter lettuce or romaine hearts, with diced celery, carrot and red onion. Celery is the classic buffalo pairing and adds crunch for almost no calories. Veg + lettuce adds ~25 cal per serving, so still ~215 cal / 28g.
  • ⚠ Sodium: ½ cup of buffalo sauce is ~3,500mg — about a day's worth in one batch, and you are on blood-pressure medication. Leaner: ¼ cup sauce stretched with water/vinegar + cayenne. Same heat, half the salt.
Thai-Style Cold Shirataki Salad CUT ~220 cal / ~12g P per serving (makes 2) — near-zero-calorie noodle, eaten cold

Built 10/17 for the low-sodium problem: normal Asian noodle flavour comes from soy / miso / fish sauce, which are all salt bombs — so this gets its flavour from acid + aromatics + a little fat instead. Under 100 mg sodium per serving as written. Price your own tofu off the package — brands run 70–130 cal per 3 oz.

  • Noodles: 2 packs shirataki, ~14 oz drained (your Liviana: 16 cal/pack)
  • Protein: 6 oz extra-firm tofu — pressed, cubed, seared
  • Veg: 1 large cucumber julienned · 1 medium carrot shredded · 2 scallions sliced · optional 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Herbs: 1 cup cilantro · ½ cup mint · ½ cup Thai basil
  • Dressing: 3 tbsp lime juice (~2 limes) · 2 cloves garlic minced · 1–2 Thai chilies minced · 1 tsp toasted sesame oil · 1 tsp rice vinegar · 2 drops liquid stevia (stands in for the palm sugar a real Thai dressing uses)
  • Method: press + cube + sear the tofu · rinse–boil–dry-fry the noodles, then chill · whisk the dressing · toss everything cold · 2 tbsp crushed unsalted peanuts on top at the very end so they stay crunchy.
  • ★ The squeak test — this is the whole technique. Rinse the noodles 1 min under cold water (the fishy smell is the packing liquid, not the noodle), boil 2–3 min, then dry-fry in a DRY pan, medium-high, until they look dry and squeak. Go by the squeak, not the clock. That step is what kills the rubbery texture and lets them take up the dressing.
  • 🍜 Fish sauce — a number, not a ban. Authentic Thai dressing runs 1–2 tbsp fish sauce per 3 tbsp lime, and this already has 3 tbsp lime, so the ratio compares directly. Per serving: 1 tsp ≈ 250mg · 2 tsp ≈ 500mg (what this card assumes) · 1 tbsp ≈ 700mg · 2 tbsp ≈ 1,400mg. With the 2 tsp the dish lands ~600 mg sodium/serving; leave it out and it is under 100. You are on blood-pressure medication, so it is a real cost — but it transforms the dish, and the call is yours.
Shiitake “Salt-Free Dashi” Bowl CUT ~180 cal / ~11g P / ~70mg sodium per serving (makes 2) — the hot one

The mushroom one. The mushroom soaking liquid becomes the broth — that is the entire idea. ~70 mg of sodium a serving is about a quarter of what one teaspoon of soy sauce would add.

  • 2 packs shirataki noodles
  • 6 DRIED shiitake mushrooms (~15 g) — dried, not fresh. Non-negotiable.
  • 3 cups water, hot but not boiling (this becomes the broth)
  • 6 oz extra-firm tofu, cubed · 2 baby bok choy, halved lengthwise
  • 3 cloves garlic sliced · 1 tbsp fresh ginger minced · ¼ tsp white pepper
  • 1 tbsp UNSEASONED rice vinegar (seasoned has added salt AND sugar) · 2 scallions sliced · 2 tsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1. Soak the dried shiitake in the 3 cups hot water — 30 min minimum, 1 hr better, overnight cold is best.
  • 2. Lift the mushrooms out with your hand — don’t pour. Grit settles on the bottom. Then pour the liquid off carefully and leave the last tablespoon behind.
  • 3. Squeeze the mushrooms out over the bowl (that squeeze is concentrated flavour), slice them, bin any woody stems.
  • 4. Noodles: rinse, boil, dry-fry until they squeak — same test as the Thai salad.
  • 5. Broth: soaking liquid + garlic + ginger + white pepper, simmer 10 min. Return the mushrooms.
  • 6. Add tofu and bok choy, simmer 3–4 min until the bok choy stems are just tender.
  • 7. Off the heat: rice vinegar, scallions, sesame oil. Acid goes in off-heat or it flattens. Noodles into the bowls, broth over.
  • Why it works with no salt: dried shiitake is rich in guanylate, and guanylate plus glutamate together taste far more savoury than either alone — which is how a salt-free broth can still read as ramen-ish. The drying is what concentrates it, so fresh shiitake will not do this.
  • Per serving: noodles 16 · shiitake 22 · tofu 80 · bok choy 10 · aromatics 14 · sesame oil 40 = ~182 cal. The tofu figure is an estimate — read your package.
Protein Pancakes ANY ~480 cal / ~58g P (whole batch, 3–4 cakes)

Blend everything, cook on medium-LOW like normal pancakes. The cottage cheese is the trick — powder alone makes them rubbery; the cheese keeps them soft.

  • 1 scoop protein isolate (chocolate works; add 1 tbsp cocoa powder to make it taste like chocolate rather than like protein)
  • 2 whole eggs + 2 whites (or ½ cup liquid whites)
  • ½ cup oats, blended to flour
  • ½ cup cottage cheese (4% — don't skip it)
  • ½ tsp baking powder · splash vanilla · pinch of salt
  • Toppings: 1 cup berries (60) + 1 tbsp real maple syrup (50) → ~590 cal / ~59g P
  • No-powder version: 3 whole eggs + ¾ cup cottage cheese + ½ cup blended oats + baking powder + vanilla → ~450 cal / ~42g P. Tastes more like a normal pancake.
  • Same batter works in a waffle iron. For French toast instead: 2 eggs + ¼ cup Fairlife + vanilla + cinnamon, 2 thick slices ≈ 400 cal / 25g P.
  • Measure the syrup once so you know what 1 tbsp looks like — the free pour is where 200 calories go.
